## 3. THE PIPELINE, exactly

    blend --(blendobj4.py in Blender)--> .obj
         --(tools_kit/obj2md3.py --axes xzy)--> .md3
         --(scratchpad/splitfit.py)--> surfaces under the cap
         --(tools_kit/namedcard.py)--> .card
         --> handfit_cards.py for hands, card2quest.py + gen_rsr.py for reloads

Blender 5.1 is at `C:\Program Files\Blender Foundation\Blender 5.1\blender.exe`.
All 38 Depot tools are in `tools_kit/`, with their `/home/claude/md3` paths
fixed. **Never write anything into RSQ_WeaponDepot** — owner's instruction,
absolute. Copy out, run here.

### Four things that were not obvious and cost hours

1. **`--axes xzy`.** These blends are Y-up. That puts bore on +X, lateral on Y,
   and the grip hanging to −Z, which is what the kit expects.
2. **The meaningful name is in different places per set.** `_ZMCwolf` names
   meshes `MP40.001` and *armatures* `Magazine`/`Trigger`/`Handle`. `_Hacx` and
   `_Meatgrinder` do the opposite — meshes `Mag`, `Frame`, `bolt`, `clip`,
   armatures `Armature.001`. `blendobj4.py` takes whichever name carries a role
   word, preferring the mesh's. Taking the armature's blindly threw `Mag` away
   on half the library.
3. **The vertex cap is blown by the exporter, not the model.** These UV layouts
   give every triangle corner a unique vertex, so 9,151 triangles become 14,444
   vertices. Smoothing does not help — it is UVs, not normals. Do **not**
   decimate: `md3_carve_surface.py` splits a body along the bore losslessly and
   self-verifies. MG42 took 7 cuts, garand 10, Shotgun 8.
4. **`namedcard.py` had a false-cylinder bug** (fixed here, `namedcard.py.orig_depot`
   is the original). `(r'cyl', 'cyl')` was a bare substring, so Blender's
   `Cylinder` became a revolver cylinder — the MG42 got four of them plus a
   `chamber`, `eject` and `load` point for a belt-fed gun. **`chambers 0` in a
   card is the tell.** Now a name made only of primitive words and digits
   returns `part`. **This fix should go back to the Depot's own copy**, but
   the owner must do that — do not write there.

### Known limits of `namedcard.py`

- It forces the **single largest surface** to be the body, by vertex count. After
  splitting a body into N pieces, the magazine can become the largest surface
  and steal the role. That is why the MG42 lost its magazine; cut its body into
  fewer, larger pieces to fix it.
- It has no `--assign`. `md3label.py` does, but `md3label` classifies by motion
  and needs multiple surfaces plus animation, so it is no use on a single-frame
  OBJ export.

## 6. Late additions, 2026-10-24 — read these before touching the pipeline

Three more bugs found after the first standard run, all in how a piece gets its
name. Each one was costing magazines.

1. **`namedcard.py` did not know the word `clip`.** The HacX UZI calls its
   magazine `clip` and its release `clipRelease`. With only `mag` in the role
   table the tool saw a magazine and called it nothing. Now `(r'mag|clip', 'mag')`,
   placed AFTER the `release|latch` line so `clipRelease` is still a latch.

2. **`blendobj4.py` let primitives block the armature fallback.** Its
   meaningful-name test used the same role word list, which contains `cyl` — so
   `Cylinder.000` looked like it carried a role and the script never fell back to
   the armature. The MG42's meshes are all `Cylinder.00N` while its *armatures*
   really are named `Magazine` and `Trigger`, so it lost a magazine it has. Now a
   name made only of primitives or filler (`Untitled`, `model.004`, `Gun.002`,
   `SMDImporter_..._reference.001`, `main_part.003_Plane.025`) falls through to
   the armature.

3. **Both fixes are in and the better run is installed.** `models/blend/` and
   `cards_blend/` now hold the output of `std2`, 34 guns, 37 MB, one convention.
   The final tally:

   | state | count | meaning |
   |---|---|---|
   | magazine named | **10** | ready for a hand reload |
   | some part named | 10 | slide, pump, bolt, trigger, latch, stock |
   | nothing named | 14 | needs a word per piece in Blender |

   With a magazine: AssaultShotgun, Chaingun, Kar98, MP40, STG44, Tommy, UZI,
   bar, cryogun, pistol. The second fix gained the UZI's magazine and `grip` on
   the MP40 and the HacX pistol.

### Naming the 14 — the loop exists, and it is NOT Blender work

I wrote earlier in this file that the unnamed guns had to be fixed in Blender by
the owner. **That was wrong.** The kit has both halves of the loop:

    tools_kit/render.py  <model.md3> 0 out.png 0
        -- draws every surface in its own colour with a legend giving its INDEX

    tools_kit/md3label.py <model.md3> <outdir> <Name> --assign 0=body,1=mag,2=trigger
        -- writes the model back with the surfaces RENAMED to the vocabulary,
           writes the .card, CARVES the assigned parts into their own files with
           a seat origin, and adds tag_mag_well / tag_muzzle / tag_grip /
           tag_support

**`--assign` works on a single-frame export.** Verified on CHAING, whose pieces
are all called `Cube` and `Cylinder`: `--assign 0=body,1=mag,2=trigger` produced
a card with `mag`, `trigger`, a `mag_well` with its mouth, and
`CHAING_mag.md3` carved out at `seat_origin [26.96, 0.23, -5.54]`. The rest of
md3label classifies by motion and can do nothing on one frame, but the assign
path does not need motion.

So the 14 are a render-then-assign pass, one gun at a time, and an agent can do
it. Renders for all 14 are in `scratchpad/renders/`. The vocabulary md3label
accepts: body slide mag round trigger hammer pump cyl bolt handle lever shell
case barrel loader sight stock grip bead.

The guns needing it: CHAING, CHAINGRPG, SSG, Saw, bfg, bolter, tec9, CANISTER,
Nuker, Stick, MG42, 1911, luger, garand.

## 7. Proven on two guns, 2026-10-24 — the naming loop in practice

The 1911 and the luger had **nothing** named: their pieces were `model.003`,
`model.004`, `SMDImporter_Mesh_v_lugerp08_p08_reference.001`. One look at
`renders_blend/<gun>.png` and one `--assign` call each gave:

    1911    body, trigger, hammer, slide, mag        + 1911_mag.md3   169 verts
    luger   body, bolt, bolt2, bolt3, trigger, mag   + luger_mag.md3  554 verts

The names are written **into the mesh**, not only into the card, and each
magazine is carved into its own model with a seat origin -- which is the thing a
hand grabs. The luger's three `bolt` pieces are its toggle action.

Count is now **12 with a magazine**, 8 with some other part, **12 still unnamed**.

## 8. READ FIRST — the 34 blend guns are NOT in the game yet

This is the gap the 2026-10-24 commit leaves. Do not read the commit as
"the new guns are in".

What exists for all 34:

| thing | where | state |
|---|---|---|
| mesh | `models/blend/<GUN>/<GUN>.md3` (+ `_mag.md3` etc.) | built, under the MD3 caps, splits lossless |
| card | `cards_blend/MS_<GUN>.card` | written by `namedcard.py` |
| render | `renders_blend/<GUN>*.png` | for naming surfaces by eye |

What does **not** exist:

- **No MODELDEF rows.** `grep -c 'models/blend/' modeldef` is **0**, and the same
  in `quest/modeldef`. The engine is never told these meshes exist, so nothing
  draws. The build packs them (`models` is in `$dirs`) — they are riding along in
  the pk3 as dead bytes.
- **No weapon class to hang them on.** A MODELDEF row needs a `(class, sprite,
  frame-char)` triple. The existing 36 rows all point at a stock weapon's
  sprite. These 34 are new guns, not replacements, so each one needs a decision:
  *which weapon does this become*, or *is it a new pickup*. That decision is the
  owner's, not a tool's.
- **No hands, no reload.** `handfit_cards.py` → `card2quest.py` → `gen_rsr.py`
  has not been run on `cards_blend/`. The cards are the input to that chain, not
  the end of it.

So the order of the remaining work is:

1. Finish naming the 12 unnamed guns (section 7) — a card with `part part part`
   cannot seat a hand or move a magazine.
2. Owner decides what each of the 34 **is** (replacement for X / new pickup).
3. Generate MODELDEF rows from that decision. Mind the 29-frame-char ceiling per
   `(class, sprite)` pair, and mind the blanking pin: a row whose sprite is a
   stock weapon's needs `MSBL A`, never the stock sprite's own letter (section 6).
4. Run the hand/reload chain on `cards_blend/`.

Until step 3 lands, the honest status of the 34 is: **measured, carded, and
sitting on disk.** Not moving.

## 9. Magazines — four were wrong, and three were tool bugs

Found by auditing every surface named `mag*` across all 34 meshes for shape and
position, then opening the render of each suspect. The numbers found the
candidates; the renders decided. Two of the seven flags were false alarms
(Tommy's magazine is correct and seated; cryogun's top cell is the author's own
name and plausible), which is the whole reason the renders are not optional.

| gun | was | is | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| MP40 | `mag` = surf2 | `mag` = surf4 | surf2 is a thin rod along the receiver. The real stick magazine is surf4, 3.8 x 2.2 x **22.0** |
| MG42 | no mag at all | `mag` = surf2 | the belt drum is the single largest surface on the gun, so it was being claimed for the body |
| Kar98 | `mag` = surf1 | `mag` = surf6, five `round` | surf1 is a cartridge. surf6 is the stripper clip. There were six surfaces called `Magazine.*` and not one was a magazine |
| Chaingun | 4 magazines | none | its feed panels are named `mag_cover` and `mag` matched as a bare substring |

### Three bugs in the tools, not in the models

1. **`namedcard.py` — `mag` matched the magazine's furniture.** `mag_cover`,
   `magwell`, `magazine_housing` are the hole, never the magazine. Now a
   negative lookahead on cover/well/hous/guide/catch/port/chute/feed/plate/lip.
2. **`namedcard.py` — the largest surface was force-claimed for the body.**
   True for most guns, false for the MG42, whose belt drum outweighs everything
   it hangs off, and meaningless once a body is split into bore-slabs. Now only
   a surface whose own name does not mark it as detachable or moving may be
   claimed. **The first version of this fix was too broad** and gave the STG44's
   34-unit receiver the role `grip`, because authors routinely name a whole gun
   `Handle` — the MP40, Kar98, Tommy and STG44 all do. Only
   mag/slide/bolt/trigger/hammer/round/case/latch/cyl/crane/ejector/pump block
   the claim now.
3. **`md3label.py` — two crashes, both from the body split.** `--assign` was
   read *after* the body was picked, so assigning a role to the largest surface
   left it with no motion record (`KeyError` on the MG42). And the grip search
   looked only at the single body surface: the Kar98's largest slab spans
   x 58..116 while its trigger sits at x 33, so three narrowing filters returned
   nothing and `argmin` died on an empty array. The body is now the union of
   every body-role slab, `--assign` is read first, and the last filter has a
   guard.

`render.py` also got its legend widened and its palette taken to 20 colours —
it wrapped at 12, so on the 15-surface garand surf12 was drawn the same red as
surf0, which is a silent way to assign the wrong index.

### Two real-world mechanics the card format cannot express

The owner's point, and it is correct: **the Kar98 and the MG42 are real weapons
and they do not load like a box-magazine gun.**

- A **Kar98k** has an internal 5-round box loaded from the **top**, through the
  open bolt, with a stripper clip that is then thrown away. There is no
  magazine to push up into a well.
- An **MG42** is **belt-fed**. The drum on the side holds the belt; you load it
  by lifting the hinged **top cover** and laying the belt in.
- The **garand** takes an **en-bloc clip**, also from the top, and ejects it.

`md3label --archetype` offers only `pistol_mag | shotgun_pump |
revolver_topbreak | shotgun_break`. Three of the most distinctive reloads in
the WW2 set have no archetype, and the cards for all three currently claim a
`mag_well` underneath the gun, which is the wrong place for every one of them.
**This needs the owner's call before the hand/reload chain runs on them.**

### Kar98: its ammunition is parked inside the buttstock

The five cartridges and the clip sit *inside the stock* at frame 0 — visible in
`renders_blend/Kar98.png` as a stack of pointed rounds in the butt. So its
computed `mag_well` lands at **x = -0.26 on a rifle 121 units long**: the very
back. The card is a faithful measurement of a model whose parts are parked. It
cannot be fixed by a tool; the parts have to be moved in the source, or the
seat overridden by hand. Same class as the 1911's dropped-out magazine
(section 7), but worse — the 1911's was at least under the grip.

### One card producer

`md3label` is used to **rename surfaces** (`--assign`) and **carve parts**; it
also writes a `<GUN>.card` of its own, which is deliberately **not** kept.
`namedcard.py` writes every card in `cards_blend/`, all 34, so one tool
measures them all the same way.

Incidental fixes caught by regenerating all 34: `MS_Knife` declared
`model MS_knife.md3` in lowercase, which would not resolve on Quest's
case-sensitive filesystem; and the 1911 and luger cards had been written by
`md3label` rather than `namedcard`, so they alone carried a different header
and no `MS_` prefix.
